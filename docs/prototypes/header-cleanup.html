Five ways to tidy the header

The header carries three things — who this is, whether the feed is alive, and what the last refresh found. Today they are laid out by one flex row with align-items: center and a gap: 1rem, which on a phone leaves live floating in the middle of nothing, breaks the status line mid-clause, and stacks three type sizes on three different rhythms. Each proposal below is the same content, relaid. Nothing in the real site has changed yet.

What ships today

For reference, and worth switching Guides on for: the dashes show what each header child actually occupies, the teal rule shows the content's left edge.

  • “live” has nothing to align to. It is centred against the whole h1 box, which on a phone is two lines tall, so the badge lands in the gap between title and subtitle.
  • The status line breaks mid-clause — CM 15/38 credits today on one line, (1180 left) orphaned on the next — because it is a single text node that wraps wherever it runs out.
  • Three rhythms in three lines. The gap: .5rem .7rem between flex lines, the subtitle's own block margin and the meta line's leading are all different, so nothing sits on a grid.
  • 🃏 sits low and wide. An emoji brings its own line box; at 1rem it pushes the title's first line down and adds a visible notch of space before the "J".
A

Tidy stack

The same anatomy, corrected. A two-column grid pins live to the title's own row so it aligns with the name's baseline, the rows share one tight rhythm, and the glyph gets a fixed line box. Nothing moves that did not need to.

What changes
ui.css only — header becomes a grid, plus a .glyph span in index.html. No app.js change: the status line stays one text node.
Wins
Smallest diff of the five, and the only one that leaves the status string alone. Alignment and rhythm are fixed at both widths.
Costs
The status line still wraps wherever it runs out of room, so “(1180 left)” can still orphan on a narrow phone.
B

Split band

Identity on the left, status on the right, both flush to their own edge. The empty middle becomes deliberate space rather than a gap something fell into, and the two right-aligned lines read as one status block.

What changes
Grid in ui.css, plus app.js emitting one <span> per clause. The middots become wide spaces — a · between clauses cannot know it has landed at the start of a line.
Wins
Strongest desktop composition — a real left and right edge, tabular numerals, and no floating badge. Reads like a status bar.
Costs
Right-alignment stops working at 390px, so the phone form has to fall back to a left-aligned third row: two layouts to maintain.
C

Status chips

The status line stops being a sentence. Each fact becomes a pill in the same vocabulary as the freshness chip on every card — so a wrap can only ever break between facts, and the credit figure gets a meter you can read without doing the division.

What changes
Most app.js work: cmSummary() returns chip markup instead of a string. New .st pill and meter in ui.css.
Wins
Scannable rather than readable — the three numbers you actually check are three separate objects, and the meter answers “how much of today is left?” at a glance.
Costs
Tallest of the five, and the chips compete with the control strip directly below them; the header stops being quiet chrome.
D

Quiet two-liner

Cut instead of arrange. The word live goes — the pulse already says it — the subtitle becomes a hairline-divided tail that drops on phones, and the status shortens to 14:01 · 316 offers · CM 15/38 (1180 left), which fits 390px on one line with room to spare.

What changes
Grid in ui.css; app.js shortens the string (time only, 15/38 without “credits today”) and the badge loses its label to a title plus visually-hidden text.
Wins
Roughly half today's header height on a phone, no wrapping at any width, and the grid starts higher up the screen.
Costs
Loses the date, the word “credits”, and the branding line on phones. A bare pulsing dot is a weaker affordance than a labelled badge.
E

Data rail

Treat the status as data, not prose: four labelled fields on a divided rail, values in tabular numerals on one baseline. The glyph becomes a tile so it reads as a mark rather than a stray character, and on a phone the rail folds to a 2×2 grid instead of wrapping.

What changes
app.js emits four label/value pairs instead of a sentence; ui.css gains the rail. The ⚿/↻ CM strip below is untouched.
Wins
Most legible numbers of the five — the micro-labels mean the values need no words around them, so nothing has to be abbreviated to fit.
Costs
The most furniture: 8 new elements and a second uppercase label style competing with the strip's SOURCE. Formal enough to feel like a different product.

My pick is B, with D's shortening applied to the phone form. The split band fixes the actual complaint — a badge aligned to nothing and a sentence that breaks in the wrong place — without inventing new furniture above a control strip that is already dense. Per-clause spans are a four-line change in app.js and they are what every option except A depends on, so they are worth doing regardless.

A is the right answer if you want this shipped in one commit and judged on a desktop. C and E both make the numbers better and the page busier; they are worth it only if the credit budget deserves permanent prominence, which the legend arguably already covers. Whichever wins, the same two invariants hold: the header keeps its opaque background and its .topbar pairing with the strip, and verify-mobile.js has to stay at zero horizontal overflow at 320px — the narrowest form, not 390px, is what constrains every one of these.
